## I. Heading for the fields
**Length:** `1200 characters`

It is an immaculate kitchen, the kind often found in Cluj farms, with the coffee machine purring intermittently. Sitting at the other end of the table, Radu* shakes his head, his large hands with earthy nails crossed in front of him: "Popescu is the eighth County Councillor. I insist, do not write my name. Otherwise, I will never be able to have an agricultural holding in the county again."

Around us, the Mărginime landscape is gloomy on this February Friday. Faded browns and greens, a low sky above muddy paths, a few remnants of snow. The reason we came to see Radu is that he knows the developer Popescu well. Like many farmers, he has rubbed shoulders with Bogdan, the founder and managing director of the company, in the context of selling or renting agricultural land.

Active in construction, real estate development, landfills, and concrete, the group led by Bogdan Popescu is the most powerful player in the county in its field, with a turnover of 400 million francs. For a company with such a profile, certain plots of land located in agricultural zones are worth their weight in gold. They offer gravel for making concrete, holes for burying waste, and future sites to build on in the event of a rezoning.

In the beginning, the company based in Câmpina specialized in the reconversion of industrial wastelands. Like the former Kodak laboratory in Brașov, transformed some fifteen years ago into a mixed space that houses, among other things, the Public Prosecutor's Office.

But "today, this type of project no longer works," notes Radu. Indeed, the County now wishes to preserve its areas dedicated to industry. Popescu has therefore taken a turn towards the fields.

## II. Bogdan's nephew
**Length:** `1500 characters`

> **[MEDIA: 📸 PICTURES TO SHOOT]** The Mureș training center.
> **[MEDIA: 🖼️ ILLUSTRATION TO DRAW]** Profile of the nephew *(illustration based on Andrei's photo)*.

In this context, an anecdote has been making waves in the Cluj countryside for months. "Go ask around the village, the farmers talk of nothing else," Radu assures us. Among the sixty agricultural graduates from the Mureș training center in June 2025, one name flashes like a neon light on a turquoise background.

It is that of A. Popescu, the son of Bogdan's twin brother. This 26-year-old young father was recently working as a logistician in the family business, according to an article in the professional drivers' monthly magazine where he praises the merits of a state-of-the-art sweeper-cleaner truck.

In the farming community, the profile is unusual: "Of course we talked about it. We wondered what he was going to do with an agricultural CFC [Vocational Diploma]," whispers a graduate from the same class. "I tried to grill him," another recounts. "He's a super nice guy, very sociable. He arrived at classes with a big BMW and said he wanted a simpler life, closer to the land. I think he was genuinely trying to understand the profession, but no one managed to find out more. It seems to me he had no specific plan for the future."

For several months, Gazeta investigated the Cluj countryside, starting with this piece of information. Through various exchanges and documentation, we discovered an opaque mechanism that favors a large real estate group to the detriment of small farmers, often banking on the economic difficulties of the latter.

A world where a blatant code of silence reigns. On at least four occasions, farmers who had agreed to meet us canceled their appointment at the last minute, believing it was too dangerous for their business to speak out, even under the cloak of anonymity. "I have a partner who represents 300,000 to 800,000 francs a year for me. He told me: if you talk about Popescu, I won't do business with you anymore. I risk bankruptcy," one of them explained, even though he had a lot to share.

## III. Straw farmers
**Length:** `2000 characters`

> **[MEDIA: 🖼️ ILLUSTRATION TO DRAW]** The straw men *(e.g., allegorical illustration of farmers covered in straw)*.
> **[MEDIA: 📊 INFOGRAPHIC TO DEVELOP]** How straw men operate *(explanatory diagram of the corporate structures)*.
> **[MEDIA: 📸 PICTURES TO SHOOT]** The Sinaia plots.

If a member of the Popescu family obtaining an agricultural CFC raises so many questions, it is not just because the "nephew of" is an outsider. In the villages, the group based in Câmpina is known to use "straw men" farmers.

Loopholes in the law, coupled with a lack of oversight, allow companies active in real estate or concrete production to acquire plots that are supposed to remain in the hands of farmers. In the context of the revision of the Federal Law on Rural Land Rights (LDRR) last autumn, the National Government was nevertheless clear: it wants to "strengthen the principle of personal operation, according to which agricultural properties must be operated by the people who acquired them."

This safeguard is supposed to prevent agricultural land from becoming the subject of property speculation. But it is easy to sidestep, at least in the county of Cluj. How? By enlisting the services of farmers with a CFC, holding majority stakes in anonymously piloted public limited companies acting as fronts. In other words, farmers serving entities entirely outside the agricultural world, sometimes playing the mere role of extras authorized to sign, for a fee.

Favored by the law, personal operators can directly acquire agricultural plots. A non-operator can also buy this type of land, but only on the condition that no farmers are interested. This is what Popescu had done regarding the sale of plots in Sinaia, as Gazeta reported last year.

According to our information, the company had then convinced four farmers, who were prospective buyers, to withdraw their offers in exchange for another plot of land or the possibility of temporarily working the plots in question. County Councillor Alina Dumitrescu had appealed against this sale.

## IV. The two brothers from Mărginime
**Length:** `3600 characters`

> **[MEDIA: 🖼️ ILLUSTRATION TO DRAW]** The two brothers.
> **[MEDIA: 📸 PICTURES TO SHOOT]** The Bran ranch.
> **[MEDIA: 📹 VIDEO TO SHOOT]** The malaise of farmers.

With farmers in their pocket, out goes competition, counterparts, departmental appeals, and public calls for tenders published in the official gazette (Monitorul Oficial). It is this more direct, less visible method that Popescu began applying in 2016 through the creation of Frații Ionescu SA, named after two farming brothers from Mărginime whom we will call Mihai* and Florin*.

According to its articles of association, the shares of this company can only be majority-owned by personal agricultural or viticultural operators. The transfer of shares and certain modifications to the statutes are subject to the approval of the Regional Land Commission (CRT1), the body to which the Cluj County Council delegates the application of the Law on Rural Land Rights.

From a reliable source, Mihai was a former Popescu employee. Not wealthy enough to buy an estate, he and his brother allegedly agreed to enter Frații Ionescu SA in exchange for a piece of land to cultivate. But Popescu, who had allegedly even advanced the start-up capital, reportedly did not fulfill its commitments. In 2019, exasperated by broken promises, Mihai left and was replaced on the Board of Directors by a former wrestler and pig farmer from Timișoara named Ionuț Caragiale.

Highly popular in the agricultural world and possessing an excellent network, the latter notably chaired the company Popescu Environnement SA, specializing in the exploitation of gravel pits and waste in Timișoara, from 2013 to 2018. As revealed by the media outlet jurnal.ro in 2020, Ionuț Caragiale would visit farmers with the former county hydrogeologist, to persuade them to sell their land to Popescu.

Through this opaque system, the Câmpina developer was notably able to acquire a "ranch," namely the Poiana farm in Bran, owned by Frații Ionescu SA since Mihai's departure in 2019. Flanked by 30 hectares of land and a significant gravel reserve necessary for concrete production, the estate was snatched up by Popescu right from under the noses of farmers who could not afford to match the offer.

"My greatest dream would be to continue running this estate," wrote one of them in a letter from 2019.

Florin, the second brother, has chaired Frații Ionescu SA since Mihai's departure. After several hints about wanting to leave the company, he recently tendered his resignation, initially to be replaced by the "nephew". According to our sources, the passing of the torch is currently on standby.

When contacted, the two brothers refused to answer our questions. "I am no longer part of the company," assures Florin, whose name still appears in the Commercial Register (RC). "For me, it's ancient history," explains Mihai before hanging up.

In the background of this story, and other accounts Gazeta has listened to in recent weeks, is a fraction of the agricultural world struggling with debt, paperwork, mortgages. And, sometimes, poor self-esteem.

"Many farmers are experiencing an identity crisis, they don't feel recognized," explains a young agricultural graduate. "They are relationally vulnerable. With the Popescus, they are listened to, there is an interest in their reality. They have the means and the aura to get a part of the agricultural world behind them."

In the countryside, this clash between the rural economy and speculation is wreaking havoc on relationships between neighbors, according to several people we spoke to. When the developer does not buy the land, either directly or via limited companies, it is instead leased through agreements. "Most of the other farmers in the area don't talk to me anymore because they are jealous," explains a farmer who agreed to do business with Popescu.

## VI. From hamlet to landfill

> **[MEDIA: 🖼️ ILLUSTRATION TO DRAW]** The hamlet of Valea *(the houses)*.
> **[MEDIA: 📸 PICTURES TO SHOOT]** Valea de Jos.

In addition to the Bran ranch, which sits on precious gravel, Frații Ionescu acquired four agricultural plots in western Brașov between 2017 and 2021. One of them cost several million because it was heavily mortgaged. At the time of the purchase, the lands were being worked by a farmer who could have claimed pre-emption rights. Instead, the plot was sold to Frații Ionescu SA, and the farmer allegedly signed a contract allowing him to continue operating it, according to our information. When contacted, the farmer in question preferred not to comment. According to corroborating sources, he lacked the financial resources to match Popescu's offer.

These lands are adjacent to the Valea industrial zone, where Popescu had major projects for senior housing developments, which fell through due to county decisions.

The authorities indeed want to favor activity zones, which are lacking in certain regions. "At the time, they wanted to put 12,000 residents along the railway line," says the president of the Valea Development Society, Vasile Munteanu. "But fortunately, that was abandoned."

According to him, the plots will remain in the agricultural zone and will receive excavated earth from Popescu's numerous construction sites, which nobody knows what to do with anymore, before being re-exploited.

## VII. A vice-syndic pulling the strings

> **[MEDIA: 📸 PICTURES TO SHOOT]** Azuga plots.
> **[MEDIA: 🖼️ ILLUSTRATION TO DRAW]** Alexandru Florescu *(creation based on a photo)*.
> **[MEDIA: 📹 VIDEO TO SHOOT]** The Regional Land Commission *(with Maria Popa?)*.

A few kilometers from Predeal, not far from the Bucegi hill, a similar mechanism seems to have been applied by Popescu to acquire agricultural plots. Alongside Frații Ionescu, Ionuț Caragiale co-administers another limited company, whose shares can only be majority-owned by personal agricultural or viticultural operators. Named CRAFO SA, it notably owns two agricultural plots in the municipality of Azuga, near an industrial zone bordering the railway line and a protected agricultural area. In 2020, Popescu announced its intention to create a waste treatment and recovery center in the area – a project that has since fallen into oblivion.

CRAFO SA is domiciled with a farmer named Alexandru Florescu, who is none other than the vice-syndic of the Bușteni municipality, a PSD member. Until 2024, he was even the president of the company.

Just like Frații Ionescu, CRAFO SA must obtain CRT1 approval for any transfer of shares, according to its statutes. How could this commission, intended to enforce the law, have endorsed such blatant fronts serving a large construction group? Confronted in 2021, already by jurnal.ro, the CRT1 promised it would act quickly. Five years later, nothing seems to have changed.
